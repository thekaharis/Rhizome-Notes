    \textbf{Abstract}\par\vspace{3pt}
    Neural operators such as the Fourier Neural Operator (FNO) learn maps between function spaces efficiently, but their kernels are global, stationary and band-limited, which makes sharp, localized structure such as the ionization fronts of the epoch of reionization hard to represent. Multi-scale hybrids like the U-Net and U-FNO recover local detail at the cost of resolution independence, and still use a fixed communication pattern. This report introduces the \emph{rhizome neural operator}, a recurrent integral operator whose connections depend on the state of the field. A learned kernel, evaluated with the FFT, is modulated at every update by per-cell source and receiver gates computed from the current hidden state, so that the effective kernel is state-dependent at the cost of a single Fourier layer. On \texttt{21cmFAST} lightcones, the rhizome outperforms the best models of an existing FNO-based pipeline on every task tested: 3-D $x_{\mathrm{HI}}$ (test RMSE 0.059 against 0.068) and joint $x_{\mathrm{HI}}$ and 21\,cm brightness temperature (0.050 / 5.8\,mK against 0.056 / 6.5\,mK). Its margin is largest on physical observables. A 2-D variant that sees $\pm32$ cells of line-of-sight context and is applied slice by slice beats the native 3-D models on the same data. It halves the error in bubble sizes and in the field-level bias, sharpens ionization fronts from 6.2 to 3.9\,Mpc, and predicts a full cone in under 6\,s. A small-scale power term in the loss removes its one weakness, excess $T_b$ structure before reionization.\par
    \vspace{8pt}
    \textbf{Keywords:} neural operators, Fourier neural operator, operator learning, 21\,cm cosmology, epoch of reionization, emulation
